Keep your Mac awake.

NoSleep is a tiny, free menu bar app that stops your Mac from going to sleep. One click on, one click off.

Download for macOS View on GitHub

Free & open source (MIT) · macOS 12 or later

How it looks

Strained eye = on

Your Mac and its display will not sleep.

Closed eye = off

Your Mac sleeps as usual.

Features

  • Starts working immediately. Open it and your Mac stays awake.
  • Timed sessions. Stay awake indefinitely, or for 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, 2 h, 4 h or 8 h, with the time remaining shown in the menu.
  • Menu bar only. No Dock icon, no windows, no network access.
  • Safe by design. Uses standard macOS power assertions, the same mechanism as caffeinate. Quit or crash and your Mac goes back to normal.
  • Light and dark menu bars. The icons adapt automatically.

Install

  1. Download NoSleep.zip and unzip it.
  2. Drag NoSleep.app into your Applications folder.
  3. Right-click the app, choose Open, then confirm. This is needed only the first time, because the app is not notarized by Apple.
  4. Optional: add it under System Settings → General → Login Items to start it at login.

Prefer to build it yourself? See the instructions on GitHub.

FAQ

How do I keep my Mac from going to sleep?

Install NoSleep and open it. It starts active right away: the menu bar icon shows a wide-open eye and your Mac will not sleep until you turn it off or quit the app.

Is NoSleep different from the caffeinate command?

It uses the same macOS power assertions as caffeinate, but as a menu bar app with one-click toggling and timed sessions, so you do not need a Terminal window.

Does it keep a MacBook awake with the lid closed?

No. Like every tool of this kind, it stops idle sleep, but closing the lid of a MacBook still puts it to sleep.

The icon is missing from my menu bar.

On Macs with a notch, macOS can hide menu bar icons under the notch when the bar is full: hold ⌘ and drag icons to make room. On macOS 26, also check that NoSleep is enabled under System Settings → Menu Bar.

Is NoSleep free?

Yes. It is free and open source under the MIT license.
